# Requirements engineering methods, writing standards and regulatory traceability: ideas for requimd

Scan date: 2026-11-18. Scope: what a plain-Markdown, git-based, offline, single-binary tool (requimd) could check, support or automate, based on RE methods, writing standards and regulated-domain traceability demands.

Source note. Most standards are paywalled (ISO, IEC, RTCA, VDA, IIBA). Where I could not open a primary or a reliable secondary source during this scan, the statement is marked **[background, unverified]**. Those statements come from general domain knowledge and must be checked before they are relied on. Edition years are given where known. No standard text is reproduced; everything is paraphrased.

Format deviation. The caller asked for one profile per source plus a final ideas table. These are added as two extra top-level sections ("Source profiles" first, "Ideas for requimd" last) around the four key-question sections, which follow the Takeaway/Cited Findings/Inferences/Gaps structure.

## Source profiles

### ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 (Requirements engineering), 2nd edition 2018

What it demands. The standard separates characteristics of individual requirements (clause 5.2.5) from characteristics of a requirement set (5.2.6), and has a clause on requirement language criteria (5.2.7) ([iTeh catalogue / sample](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/8cf2bc2b-8b5e-4907-a82a-d1c5676c9e85/iso-iec-ieee-29148-2018); [ANSI preview](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/BSI/preview_30357521.pdf)). Individual requirements should be necessary, appropriate, unambiguous, complete, singular, feasible, verifiable, correct, conforming and traceable ([arXiv 2502.18617](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18617)). The standard gives little guidance on verifiability beyond saying it improves when a requirement is measurable (same source). Set characteristics are complete, consistent, feasible, comprehensible and able to be validated **[background, unverified]**. The language criteria warn against superlatives, subjective words, vague pronouns, ambiguous adjectives and adverbs, open-ended terms ("including but not limited to"), comparatives, loopholes ("if possible", "as appropriate") and negative statements **[background, unverified]**. The recommended attributes include identification, stakeholder priority, dependency, risk, source, rationale, difficulty and type **[background, unverified]**. The information items are BRS (business), StRS (stakeholder), SyRS (system), SRS (software), plus ConOps and OpsCon **[background, unverified]**.

What a tool can do. Lint the language criteria with word lists (requimd already does this). Make attributes such as rationale, source and verification method required per item type. Check that each item carries a trace link. Map requimd item types onto the 29148 information items (BR to BRS, UC/FR/NFR to StRS/SRS), so that an "SRS view" can be exported in the order of the 29148 outline.

### INCOSE Guide to Writing Requirements (GtWR) v4, June 2023, and Guide to Needs and Requirements (GtNR)

What it demands. GtWR v4 is INCOSE-TP-2010-006-04, published June 2023 ([Jama summary](https://www.jamasoftware.com/requirements-management-guide/writing-requirements/incose-requirements-writing-guide/); [INCOSE v4 summary sheet](https://www.incose.org/docs/default-source/working-groups/requirements-wg/guidetowritingrequirements/incose_rwg_gtwr_v4_summary_sheet.pdf), which returned 403 to automated fetches). It defines 15 characteristics (C1 to C9 for individual needs and requirements, C10 to C15 for sets) and 42 rules, and maps each rule to the characteristics it supports ([Jama](https://www.jamasoftware.com/requirements-management-guide/writing-requirements/incose-requirements-writing-guide/); [se-trends review](https://www.se-trends.de/en/review-incose-guide-for-writing-requirements/)). A third-party open workbench says it implements all 42 v4 rules in 14 categories as automated checks ([INCOSE requirements workbench](https://ssaleem74.github.io/incose-requirements-workbench/)). Confirmed rules include R1 structured statements, R2 active voice, R4 defined terms, R7 vague terms, R8 escape clauses, R16 use of "not", R18 single-thought statements, R19 combinators, R31 solution-free (implementation only with rationale), R34 measurable performance, R36 consistent terms and units, and R40 decimal format, new in v4 ([Jama](https://www.jamasoftware.com/requirements-management-guide/writing-requirements/incose-requirements-writing-guide/)). The guide covers writing only, not elicitation or analysis ([se-trends](https://www.se-trends.de/en/review-incose-guide-for-writing-requirements/)). A January 2026 INCOSE webinar refers to a "GfWR" (Guide for Writing Requirements), which suggests a renamed or newer edition ([INCOSE webinar 144](https://www.incose.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/webinar_144_the_incose_gfwr_raising_the_ante.pdf)). Its content was not checked.

What a tool can do. GtWR is the best catalogue to anchor requimd rule IDs in: each lint message could cite the INCOSE rule (for example "R7 vague term"). The section "Which quality rules can be checked automatically" below sorts the rules into deterministic and judgement-based.

### EARS (Mavin et al., Rolls-Royce, RE'09)

What it demands. EARS was first published in 2009 and grew out of work on airworthiness regulations for a jet-engine control system ([alistairmavin.com/ears](https://alistairmavin.com/ears/)). Its generic syntax is an optional precondition, then an optional trigger, then the system name, then "shall" and the response. The clauses always come in that fixed order, with zero or more preconditions, zero or one trigger, exactly one system name and one or more responses (same source). The patterns are ubiquitous (no keyword), state-driven (While), event-driven (When), optional feature (Where), unwanted behaviour (If ... then), and complex (a combination) (same source). EARS is used in AI spec-driven development tooling from 2025 on ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easy_Approach_to_Requirements_Syntax)).

What a tool can do. Parse EARS deterministically: classify each requirement by pattern, check clause order, at most one trigger, exactly one system name (checked against a glossary list of system names), and an If without a then. Show the pattern as a semantic token or hover. Report pattern statistics, for example "no unwanted-behaviour requirements for component X", which is a hint at missing error handling. Offer code actions that insert EARS skeletons. German keywords need a mapping table (see the German key question).

### SOPHIST / Rupp MASTeR templates (German-speaking practice)

What it demands. The Rupp/SOPHIST sentence template orders a requirement as: condition, system, priority (modal verb), system activity type, object and additions, process verb ([LMU handout "Formulierungsregel Rupp Schablone"](https://www.pst.ifi.lmu.de/Lehre/fruhere-semester/sose-2009/seprakt/Formulierungsregel_DE_Rupp_Schablone.pdf)). The modal verb carries legal weight and priority: "muss" (binding), "soll" (strongly recommended), "wird" (future, intention) (same source). There are three activity types: independent system activity (no addition), user interaction ("<wem> die Möglichkeit bieten, ..."), and interface requirement ("fähig sein, ...") ([LMU handout](https://www.pst.ifi.lmu.de/Lehre/fruhere-semester/sose-2009/seprakt/Formulierungsregel_DE_Rupp_Schablone.pdf); [Speakerdeck "Requirements 101"](https://speakerdeck.com/donschado/requirements-101-de)). Conditions start with "wenn" (temporal) or "falls" (logical), and the process verb comes last as an infinitive (LMU handout). SOPHIST also has a rule set based on the linguistic effects deletion, generalisation and distortion (Tilgung, Generalisierung, Verzerrung) for analysing statements ([Speakerdeck](https://speakerdeck.com/donschado/requirements-101-de)). A KIT thesis built requirements from SOPHIST templates by extracting semantic roles and reports better completeness ([KIT thesis](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000100411/50859240)). SOPHIST co-founded IREB and runs more than 300 trainings a year ([SOPHIST training catalogue](https://www.sophist.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Bilder_zu_Seiten/Training/SOPHIST_Katalog_Offene_Trainings_2024ALT3.pdf)). The current MASTeR family (FunktionsMASTeR, EigenschaftsMASTeR, UmgebungsMASTeR, ProzessMASTeR) and its exact slots were not confirmed in public sources **[background, unverified]**.

What a tool can do. A German template parser: find the modal verb (muss/soll/wird/kann), map it to the `prio` metadata and flag inconsistency (for example "soll" in the text with `prio: must`). Classify the activity type from "die Möglichkeit bieten" and "fähig sein". Check that the infinitive comes last (German verb-final order). Check that the condition comes first with "wenn" or "falls". Detect nominalisations ("-ung", "-heit", "-keit" nouns used where a process verb belongs), which is the SOPHIST deletion symptom.

### Planguage (Tom Gilb)

What it demands. Quality requirements are stated with a Scale (unit of measure), a Meter (how to measure), and levels: Past (benchmark), Tolerable (constraint level, possibly several by condition), Goal (the target to meet) and Wish (desired, possibly unattainable) ([Modern Analyst](https://modernanalyst.com/Resources/Articles/tabid/115/ID/2926/Specifying-Quality-Requirements-With-Planguage.aspx); [ArgonDigital](https://argondigital.com/blog/product-management/specifying-quality-requirements-with-planguage); [Gilb ICEIS 2004 tutorial](https://iceis.scitevents.org/ICEIS2004/tutorials/gilb%20tut.pdf)). An example from Gilb: usability productivity with Past 65 minutes, Tolerable 35, Goal 25 (Gilb tutorial).

What a tool can do. Support Planguage keywords as structured metadata lines on NFR items (`scale:`, `meter:`, `past:`, `tolerable:`, `goal:`, `wish:`). Check deterministically that an NFR has scale, meter and goal; that the values carry units; that they are numerically ordered (Tolerable between Past and Goal in the direction of improvement); and that the units match the glossary. This turns "NFR is measurable" into a mechanical check.

### Volere requirements template (Robertson and Robertson)

What it demands **[background, unverified; searches returned no relevant pages]**. Every atomic requirement ("snow card", originally an index card) has: requirement number, type (template section), event/use case numbers, description, rationale, originator (source), fit criterion, customer satisfaction and dissatisfaction ratings (1 to 5), priority, conflicts, supporting materials and history. The fit criterion is a measurable, testable statement that makes the requirement verifiable. The Volere template also has a fixed section structure (project drivers, constraints, functional, non-functional with subtypes, project issues).

What a tool can do. Provide a "Volere profile": required fields per item (rationale, originator, fit criterion). Report items without a fit criterion; this is the same check as requimd's missing acceptance criteria. Add a `conflicts` link type with a symmetric check: if A conflicts with B, B should list A, or the tool infers it. Sort by satisfaction and dissatisfaction. Map the NFR subtypes to tags.

### BABOK v3 (IIBA, 2015) - Requirements Life Cycle Management

What it demands. Chapter 5 has five tasks: Trace, Maintain, Prioritize, Assess Requirements Changes, Approve ([IIBA task cards](https://www.iiba.org/contentassets/a8cf501643fb4feaac7c308e152eb03f/all-task-cards-req-and-designs-life-cycle-mgmt.pdf); [Watermark Learning](https://watermarklearning.com/blog/babok-requirements-life-cycle-management/amp)). The tasks are not sequential and cover designs as well as requirements, "from inception to retirement" ([Modern Requirements](https://www.modernrequirements.com/blogs/requirements-lifecycle-management-guide/); [microtool](https://www.microtool.de/en/knowledge-base/requirements-in-business-analysis/)). Prioritisation weighs value, urgency, dependencies and risk. Change assessment uses impact analysis. Approve was new in v3 (same secondary sources). BABOK's trace relationships are derive, depends (necessity, effort), satisfy and validate **[background, unverified]**. BABOK perspectives (Agile, BI, IT, Business Architecture, BPM) shape which artefacts dominate **[background]**. An IIBA BABOK v4 was not confirmed in this scan (see Gaps).

What a tool can do. Lifecycle states with allowed transitions (draft, review, approved, retired) checked in CI. "Approve" as an auditable event: the approval comes from a merged PR or a signed tag, never from the tool itself, which matches BR-004. A change-impact report (suspect links, reverse-trace closure). A reuse marker. Trace kinds aligned to BABOK (derive, depends, satisfy, validate).

### IREB CPRE Foundation Level (syllabus 3.x)

What it demands. The English syllabus v3.2.0 is dated 26 February 2024. IREB's downloads page also lists v3.1.0 valid from 1 August 2025, and there are 3.2.x variants (3.2.1 BR edition, 3.2.2 KO), so the edition numbering is inconsistent across sources ([IREB downloads](https://www.ireb.org/en/downloads/); [gasq syllabus EN 3.2](https://gasq.org/files/content/gasq/downloads/certification/IREB/cpre_foundationlevel_syllabus_en_v.3.2.pdf); [German syllabus 3.2](https://cockpit-v1.ireb.org/media/pages/downloads/cpre-foundation-level-syllabus/829790aebe-1733311657/cpre_foundationlevel_syllabus_de_v.3.2.pdf)). Foundation Level covers work products in natural-language, template-based and model-based form ([IREB foundation level](https://cpre.ireb.org/en/concept/foundationlevel)). Requirements management has its own Advanced/Practitioner module ([IREB RM factsheet](https://ireb.org/content/1-cpre/5-advanced/factsheet-cpre-management-en.pdf)). The FL content includes quality criteria for work products and for requirements; templates (phrase templates such as EARS and Rupp, form templates such as user stories and use case forms, document templates such as 29148 and Volere); glossaries; validation techniques (inspection, walkthrough, perspective-based reading, prototypes); attributes and views; versioning, configurations and baselines; change control; traceability; and prioritisation **[background, unverified against 3.2 text]**.

What a tool can do. IREB is the curriculum most German-speaking BAs know. requimd documentation and rule names could use IREB terms (Arbeitsprodukt, Satzschablone, Glossar, Baseline, Konfiguration). Baselines and configurations map directly to git tags plus the lock file.

### ISO/IEC 25010:2023 and quality attribute scenarios

What it demands. ISO/IEC 25010:2023 (edition 2, November 2023) replaces the 2011 edition. Its product quality model has nine characteristics with subcharacteristics ([ISO 78176](https://www.iso.org/standard/78176.html); [standard.no](https://online.standard.no/en/isoiec-25010-2023)). The nine are functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, interaction capability (formerly usability), reliability, security, maintainability, flexibility (formerly portability) and safety (new) **[background; names consistent with search snippets but not read from the standard]**. Quality attribute scenarios (SEI; Bass, Clements, Kazman) have six parts: source of stimulus, stimulus, environment, artifact, response, response measure **[background, unverified]**.

What a tool can do. A controlled `quality:` attribute on NFRs whose values come from the 25010 vocabulary (characteristic.subcharacteristic). A coverage report "NFRs by 25010 characteristic" that shows blind spots (for example no safety or compatibility NFRs). A scenario form with the six fields, checked for completeness, where response measure must contain a number and a unit. This links naturally to Planguage.

### Use cases, user stories, acceptance criteria, mapping methods

What they demand **[background, unverified in this scan]**.
- Cockburn use cases: a fully dressed template with scope, level (summary, user goal, subfunction), primary actor, stakeholders and interests, preconditions, minimal guarantees, success guarantees, trigger, main success scenario as numbered steps, and extensions keyed to step numbers (for example 3a). Writing guidance: each step has an actor and an action, goal level as "sea level", no UI detail.
- User stories: "As a <role> I want <capability> so that <benefit>". INVEST (Bill Wake, 2003): Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable.
- Gherkin acceptance criteria: Given/When/Then with And/But, Scenario Outline with Examples tables. The Gherkin keywords are localised, including German (Angenommen/Gegeben sei, Wenn, Dann, Und, Aber).
- Story mapping (Jeff Patton): a backbone of user activities, then tasks, then stories sliced into releases.
- Impact mapping (Gojko Adzic): Goal, Actors, Impacts, Deliverables tree.
- Jobs-to-be-done "job story": "When <situation>, I want to <motivation>, so I can <expected outcome>".

What a tool can do. Most of these are structural and checkable. Extension labels must reference existing step numbers (requimd already has step labels, BR-003). Use case level and primary actor present. Actor names defined in the glossary. Story role in the glossary. "so that" clause present. Gherkin blocks parsed and validated. INVEST: only Testable (acceptance criteria present) and partly Small (step count or size heuristics) and Independent (count of depends-links) can be checked mechanically. Story map and impact map can be derived views if items carry `activity:` or `goal:` links, then exported as Mermaid.

### Regulatory and safety traceability

**Automotive SPICE 4.0 (VDA QMC, 2023).** SYS.2 (and SWE.1 and the other engineering processes) require bidirectional traceability and consistency between stakeholder requirements, system requirements and downstream items. The links support coverage, consistency and impact analysis ([UL ASPICE pocket guide](https://www.ul.com/sites/default/files/2024-10/Automotive_Spice_Pocket_Guide.pdf); [UL SYS.2 article](https://www.ul.com/sis/insights/automotive-spice-system-requirements-analysis-sys2); [itemis glossary](https://www.itemis.com/en/glossary/automotive-spice/)). Sources disagree on BP numbering: the pocket guide puts it at BP5-6 for 4.0, while others use BP6/BP7 for 3.1 or BP4 ([UL pocket guide](https://www.ul.com/sites/default/files/2024-10/Automotive_Spice_Pocket_Guide.pdf) vs [ease](https://go.easesolutions.com/2025-07-01-aspice-blog-part-3)). 4.0 raises the formal demands for evidence chains across V-model levels ([itemis](https://www.itemis.com/en/glossary/requirements-traceability/)). 4.0 also speaks of "verification measures" rather than test cases, and of a third "consistency" dimension often called tri-directional traceability ([LinkedIn, Klaus Weber](https://de.linkedin.com/in/klausweber), title only). Assessors want link coverage reports, consistency evidence (reviews) and impact analysis records.

**ISO 26262:2018 (2nd ed.).** Part 8 clause 6 specifies safety requirements. **[Background, unverified]**: each safety requirement carries a unique identifier, a status and an ASIL. Safety requirements must be atomic, unambiguous, consistent, verifiable and traceable (to the upper safety goal or requirement and to verification). Configuration management (Part 8 clause 7) and change management (clause 8) require baselines and change impact analysis. Tools used for requirements may need tool confidence classification (TCL), which vendors describe as parallel to DO-330 tool qualification ([Rapita](https://www.rapitasystems.com/products/features/tool-qualification-do-178-and-iso-26262)).

**DO-178C (RTCA, 2011) / DO-330.** Traceability is required at all software levels D to A, and Level A adds source-to-object-code traceability ([Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/embedded/article/21794228/do-178c-enhances-safety-critical-avionics-software-development)). Tool qualification follows DO-330 ([Rapita](https://www.rapitasystems.com/products/features/tool-qualification-do-178-and-iso-26262)). **[Background, unverified]**: DO-178C introduced "trace data" as an explicit life-cycle data item. Derived requirements (not traceable to a higher level) must be identified and fed back to the system safety assessment. High-level and low-level requirements are reviewed against checklists (accuracy, consistency, verifiability, conformance to standards, traceability). Baselines and problem reports fall under the software configuration management process.

**IEC 62304 (2006 + A1:2015) and FDA QMSR.** IEC 62304 builds traceability into the process: development planning must ensure it (5.1.1) and verification must show it (5.7.1) ([Greenlight Guru](https://www.greenlight.guru/blog/iec-62304-software-dhf); [DQS](https://www.dqsglobal.com/en/explore/blog/iec62304-traceability-framework)). A second edition is in preparation; sources disagree on whether it is expected in 2026, and its publication is not confirmed ([intuitionlabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/iec-62304-vs-fda-csa); [Greenlight Guru](https://www.greenlight.guru/blog/iec-62304-software-dhf)). FDA's QMSR took effect on 2 February 2026 and incorporates ISO 13485:2016 into 21 CFR 820. Design traceability therefore follows ISO 13485 clause 7.3 (outputs traceable to inputs), and the "design and development file" (DDF) replaces the old DHF term ([Greenlight Guru](https://www.greenlight.guru/blog/iec-62304-software-dhf); [meddeviceguide](https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/traceability-matrix-medical-devices-guide)). The typical chain is user needs, then requirements, then risk controls, then verification and validation, and none of the standards prescribes how the records are stored (same sources). Records that need electronic signatures fall under 21 CFR Part 11 **[background, unverified]**.

**EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) / IEC 62443.** **[Background, unverified; not fetched]**: the CRA sets essential cybersecurity requirements (Annex I) for products with digital elements. Technical documentation must show how they are met, and SBOMs are expected. Reporting obligations apply from September 2026 and most obligations from December 2027 ([EUR-Lex 2024/2847](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj), not opened). IEC 62443-4-1 (secure development lifecycle) requires security requirements to be traced to threats and to security verification, and 62443-3-3 or 4-2 provide catalogues of numbered security requirements (SR / CR). Relevance to requimd: a catalogue of external requirements (CRA Annex I items, 62443 CRs) imported as read-only items that project requirements `satisfy`, plus a compliance coverage matrix.

What a tool can do (all regulated domains). Generate evidence from git rather than holding a database: a baseline (tag plus lock file), a traceability report at a baseline, a coverage report (every requirement has a `ver` link and every TC traces up), a derived-requirement report (items without a parent), suspect-link reports as change-impact evidence, a per-item change history from `git log`, review evidence (PR approvals, or `Reviewed-by:` commit trailers), and signatures (signed tags or commits). Tool qualification argument: deterministic output plus a self-test fixture suite (`examples/broken/EXPECTED.md`) is the raw material for a TCL/DO-330 qualification kit.

### Interchange and linking standards

**ReqIF 1.2 (OMG formal/16-07-01, July 2016).** Formal version 1.2 ([OMG ReqIF](https://www.omg.org/spec/ReqIF)). Attribute definitions sit on a SpecObjectType and are shared by all objects of that type. Relation attributes sit on a SpecRelationType ([Eclipse RMF Q&A](https://eclipse.org/lists/rmf-dev/pdf4ov4UENSnk.pdf)). The ProSTEP iViP Implementation Guide v1.10 (January 2024) is the practical profile for tool interoperability and describes the 1.0.1-to-1.2 differences ([ProSTEP guide](https://www.prostep.org/fileadmin/prod-download/ProSTEP_iViP_ImplementationGuide_ReqIF_V1-10_aw.pdf)). There are open issues on SpecRelation source and target clarity ([OMG issues](https://issues.omg.org/issues/spec/ReqIF)). **[Background]**: rich text is carried as an XHTML subset, hierarchy as SpecHierarchy trees, and ReqIF is the de facto exchange route with OEMs using DOORS or Polarion in automotive.

**OSLC RM / QM (OASIS Open Project).** **[Background, unverified; not fetched]**: OSLC RM 2.1 defines Requirement and RequirementCollection with link properties such as elaboratedBy, specifiedBy, affectedBy, trackedBy, implementedBy, validatedBy, satisfiedBy/satisfies, decomposedBy/decomposes and constrainedBy/constrains. OSLC QM has TestCase, TestPlan and TestResult with validatesRequirement. OSLC is a REST/linked-data protocol, so a live server breaks requimd's offline single-binary model, but its link vocabulary is a useful reference for naming link kinds, and a static RDF/JSON-LD export would be possible ([open-services.net](https://open-services.net/), not opened).

**SPDX / CycloneDX.** Relevant only at the edges. **[Background]**: CycloneDX 1.5+ can describe "formulation" and attestations, and CycloneDX 1.6 added an attestations model (CDXA) for mapping standards requirements to claims and evidence, which could carry "requirement X satisfied by evidence Y" ([cyclonedx.org](https://cyclonedx.org/), not opened). This could matter for CRA evidence.

What a tool can do. ReqIF export (and possibly import) as an offline file transform. A JSON-LD export using OSLC RM terms. Optional CycloneDX attestation export for compliance catalogues.

### Validation techniques: inspections, checklists, perspective-based reading, prototyping

What they demand **[background, unverified]**. Inspections (Fagan) use roles (moderator, author, reader, inspectors, scribe), entry and exit criteria, checklists and defect logs. Perspective-based reading (Basili et al., 1996) gives each reviewer a perspective (user, designer, tester) with a scenario: the tester derives test cases, the designer sketches a design, the user writes a user manual outline. The IREB FL lists inspection, walkthrough, perspective-based reading and prototyping as validation techniques. Prototypes (throw-away, evolutionary; low to high fidelity) validate requirements with stakeholders.

What a tool can do. Generate review packages: the set of changed items since a baseline (diff) plus a checklist rendered from the rules that cannot be automated, one checklist per perspective. Record review results as Markdown review records in the repo (review ID, items with content hash, reviewers, findings, verdict) that the lock file can validate: "review R-12 covered FR-010 at hash abc; FR-010 changed since, so the review is stale". Prototype links (`proto:` link to a file or URL) as a validation trace.

## Which quality rules can be checked automatically and deterministically (without AI) and which need judgement?

### Takeaway
Roughly half the INCOSE rules and most 29148 language criteria are lexical or syntactic and can be checked with word lists, patterns and a light part-of-speech heuristic. The characteristics that make a requirement good (necessary, correct, feasible, appropriate, set completeness) need human judgement. A tool can only prompt for them with checklists and structural proxies.

### Cited Findings
- GtWR v4 (June 2023) has 42 rules mapped to 15 characteristics. A rule supports one or more characteristics; it does not prove them — [Jama](https://www.jamasoftware.com/requirements-management-guide/writing-requirements/incose-requirements-writing-guide/); [se-trends](https://www.se-trends.de/en/review-incose-guide-for-writing-requirements/)
- A third-party open-source workbench claims all 42 v4 rules in 14 categories as automated checks. That claim covers heuristic checks, not proof of the characteristics — [INCOSE workbench](https://ssaleem74.github.io/incose-requirements-workbench/)
- Confirmed rule titles include R1 structured statements, R2 active voice, R4 defined terms, R7 vague terms, R8 escape clauses, R16 "not", R18 single thought, R19 combinators, R31 solution-free, R34 measurable performance, R36 consistent terms and units, and R40 decimal format — [Jama](https://www.jamasoftware.com/requirements-management-guide/writing-requirements/incose-requirements-writing-guide/)
- 29148 says verifiability improves when a requirement is measurable but gives little further guidance — [arXiv 2502.18617](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18617)
- EARS gives deterministic structural rules: fixed clause order, 0..n preconditions, 0..1 trigger, exactly one system name, 1..n responses — [alistairmavin.com](https://alistairmavin.com/ears/)
- Rupp template: the modal verb encodes bindingness, activity types are signalled by fixed phrases ("die Möglichkeit bieten", "fähig sein"), conditions start with "wenn" or "falls", and the process verb comes last — [LMU handout](https://www.pst.ifi.lmu.de/Lehre/fruhere-semester/sose-2009/seprakt/Formulierungsregel_DE_Rupp_Schablone.pdf)
- Planguage parameters (Scale, Meter, Past, Tolerable, Goal, Wish) are explicit fields, so their presence and ordering can be checked mechanically — [Modern Analyst](https://modernanalyst.com/Resources/Articles/tabid/115/ID/2926/Specifying-Quality-Requirements-With-Planguage.aspx)

## Which attributes and relationship kinds do the standards recommend?

### Takeaway
The standards agree on a small core of attributes: ID, type, status, priority, source/originator, rationale, verification method or fit criterion, owner/stakeholder, risk/criticality, and version or history. Safety standards add an integrity level (ASIL, DAL, software safety class). Relationship kinds converge on derive/refine (vertical), satisfy (design to requirement), verify/validate (test to requirement), depends, conflicts and constrains.

### Cited Findings
- 29148 has dedicated clauses for individual and set characteristics; traceability is one of the individual characteristics — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18617); [iTeh](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/8cf2bc2b-8b5e-4907-a82a-d1c5676c9e85/iso-iec-ieee-29148-2018)
- GtWR R31: implementation-specific statements only with a rationale, which makes rationale a functional attribute, not decoration — [Jama](https://www.jamasoftware.com/requirements-management-guide/writing-requirements/incose-requirements-writing-guide/)
- BABOK RLCM: trace, maintain (reuse), prioritise (value, urgency, dependencies, risk), assess changes (impact analysis), approve; it covers designs as well as requirements — [IIBA task cards](https://www.iiba.org/contentassets/a8cf501643fb4feaac7c308e152eb03f/all-task-cards-req-and-designs-life-cycle-mgmt.pdf); [Modern Requirements](https://www.modernrequirements.com/blogs/requirements-lifecycle-management-guide/)
- Rupp modal verbs map to bindingness/priority (muss, soll, wird) — [LMU handout](https://www.pst.ifi.lmu.de/Lehre/fruhere-semester/sose-2009/seprakt/Formulierungsregel_DE_Rupp_Schablone.pdf)
- ASPICE: bidirectional traceability between stakeholder and system requirements and further down, supporting coverage, consistency and impact analysis — [UL](https://www.ul.com/sis/insights/automotive-spice-system-requirements-analysis-sys2)
- ReqIF models attributes per SpecObjectType and SpecRelationType, so link kinds are typed and can carry attributes — [Eclipse RMF](https://eclipse.org/lists/rmf-dev/pdf4ov4UENSnk.pdf)
- Medical: the chain runs user needs, then requirements, then risk controls, then verification and validation — [Greenlight Guru](https://www.greenlight.guru/blog/iec-62304-software-dhf)

### Inferences
- A consolidated attribute set for requimd profiles. Core: `id`, `type`, `status`, `prio`. Recommended: `source`/`originator`, `rationale`, `verification method` (one of inspection, analysis, demonstration, test, the classic IADT set **[background]**), `owner`, `stakeholder`. Domain extensions: `stability`/volatility, `risk`, `asil`/`dal`/`safety-class`, `release`/`iteration`, `quality` (25010 characteristic), `satisfaction`/`dissatisfaction` (Volere).
- Relationship kinds for requimd's typed links, with sources: `derives`/`refines` (29148, INCOSE parent/child, ASPICE, DO-178C HLR to LLR), `satisfies` (BABOK, OSLC; needed for the design level), `ver`/`validates` (BABOK, OSLC QM, every safety standard), `depends` (BABOK), `conflicts` (Volere), `constrains` (OSLC, NFR to FR), `mitigates` (risk control, ISO 14971/IEC 62304), `implements` (code), `elaborates` (UC to FR, OSLC). Derived-requirement flag: an item with no parent that must say so explicitly (DO-178C, **[background]**).
- Making attributes profile-dependent ("29148", "Volere", "ASPICE", "IEC 62304") keeps the default lightweight for a business analyst while allowing regulated teams to require more.

### Gaps
- The exact attribute list of 29148 (clause 5.2.8 or similar) and of ISO 26262-8 clause 6 could not be verified.
- The OSLC RM 2.1 / 3.0 property names were not verified against the OASIS spec in this scan.

## What evidence do regulated domains require that requimd could generate from git history and its lock file?

### Takeaway
Regulated domains need the same five evidence types: (1) identified baselines, (2) bidirectional trace and coverage reports at a baseline, (3) change history with impact analysis, (4) review/approval records tied to exact content, and (5) for some, signatures and tool confidence. Git plus a content-hash lock file can produce (1) to (3) almost entirely. It can produce (4) and (5) if review results and signatures are captured in the repository (trailers, review files, signed tags) rather than only in a hosting platform.

### Cited Findings
- ASPICE: bidirectional traceability supports coverage, consistency and impact analysis; 4.0 raises formal demands on evidence chains across V-model levels — [UL](https://www.ul.com/sis/insights/automotive-spice-system-requirements-analysis-sys2); [itemis](https://www.itemis.com/en/glossary/requirements-traceability/)
- DO-178C: traceability required at levels D to A; Level A adds source-to-object-code — [Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/embedded/article/21794228/do-178c-enhances-safety-critical-avionics-software-development)
- Tool qualification: DO-330 for DO-178C, and tool confidence levels under ISO 26262; qualification status matters in practice for traceability tools — [Rapita](https://www.rapitasystems.com/products/features/tool-qualification-do-178-and-iso-26262); [arXiv 2511.11550](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11550v1)
- IEC 62304 requires traceability in planning (5.1.1) and in verification (5.7.1). FDA QMSR (effective 2 February 2026) brings ISO 13485 clause 7.3 traceability of design outputs to inputs, and the DDF replaces the DHF term — [Greenlight Guru](https://www.greenlight.guru/blog/iec-62304-software-dhf); [DQS](https://www.dqsglobal.com/en/explore/blog/iec62304-traceability-framework)
- The standards do not prescribe how records are stored — [Greenlight Guru](https://www.greenlight.guru/blog/iec-62304-software-dhf)
- BABOK's Approve task is a distinct lifecycle step — [Watermark Learning](https://watermarklearning.com/blog/babok-requirements-life-cycle-management/amp)

### Inferences
- **Baseline report:** `requimd baseline <tag>` produces a frozen bundle: item list with content hashes (from the lock file), status counts, trace matrix, coverage, open suspect links, and the git commit or tag ID. Comparing two baselines produces a "change since last baseline" report with impact (suspect closure).
- **Per-item history:** derived from `git log -L` or a diff of item blocks across commits. It shows who changed which item when, and the commit messages, which carry requirement IDs by convention (as this project already does).
- **Review records:** a `reviews/` folder of Markdown records (or commit trailers such as `Reviewed-by:` and `Approved-by:`) listing item IDs with the hashes they reviewed. The lock file can then mark a review as stale when an item changes after review. That is the requirement-level analogue of suspect links, and it is exactly what an assessor asks for ("was the released version the reviewed version?").
- **Approvals and signatures:** signed git tags or commits (GPG/SSH) as approval signatures. requimd can verify that a baseline tag is signed and list signers, but must never set `approved` itself (consistent with BR-004). Whether signed tags satisfy 21 CFR Part 11 is a legal and organisational question, not a tool feature.
- **Coverage and derived-requirement reports:** list items without a parent (derived, must be flagged and justified), items without a `ver` link, and tests without a requirement (orphan tests). These are standard DO-178C, ASPICE and IEC 62304 audit questions.
- **Tool confidence kit:** a documented rule catalogue, deterministic behaviour, the fixture corpus with expected diagnostics, and versioned releases support a TCL/TQL argument. This is a documentation and test deliverable, not new runtime code.
- **External requirement catalogues:** regulated teams must show compliance with clause lists (CRA Annex I, IEC 62443 CRs, IEC 62304 clauses). Read-only catalogue items plus `satisfies` links give a compliance matrix with the same machinery.

### Gaps
- Exact ISO 26262-8 clause 7/8 and DO-178C section 7 configuration-management evidence lists were not read (paywalled). No reliable public source was found in this scan on baselines and problem reporting specifics.
- CRA documentation obligations and dates were not verified from EUR-Lex in this scan.
- Whether git-based evidence has been accepted by assessors in ASPICE, FDA or DO-178C audits: no public case study found.

## What do German-speaking analysts commonly use, and how would checks differ in German?

### Takeaway
German-speaking analysts are shaped by IREB CPRE (co-founded by SOPHIST) and by Rupp's sentence templates (muss/soll/wird plus activity type plus infinitive at the end), and they work with the Lastenheft/Pflichtenheft split. German checks differ mainly in word order (verb-final, separable verbs), morphology (inflection and compounds affect glossary matching), the modal-verb semantics, the German passive (werden + Partizip II), and nominalisation as a typical defect.

### Cited Findings
- SOPHIST co-founded IREB and runs more than 300 trainings a year with more than 2,500 participants — [SOPHIST catalogue](https://www.sophist.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Bilder_zu_Seiten/Training/SOPHIST_Katalog_Offene_Trainings_2024ALT3.pdf)
- The Rupp template uses the modal verbs muss, soll, wird; the activity phrases "die Möglichkeit bieten" and "fähig sein"; "wenn" (temporal) and "falls" (logical); and a process verb in the infinitive at the end — [LMU handout](https://www.pst.ifi.lmu.de/Lehre/fruhere-semester/sose-2009/seprakt/Formulierungsregel_DE_Rupp_Schablone.pdf)
- The SOPHIST rule set targets deletion, generalisation and distortion — [Speakerdeck](https://speakerdeck.com/donschado/requirements-101-de)
- An IREB German syllabus (3.2) exists alongside the English one — [IREB DE syllabus](https://cockpit-v1.ireb.org/media/pages/downloads/cpre-foundation-level-syllabus/829790aebe-1733311657/cpre_foundationlevel_syllabus_de_v.3.2.pdf)
- SOPHIST template-based structuring improved completeness in a KIT study — [KIT thesis](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000100411/50859240)

### Inferences
- **Modal verbs:** in German "soll" is weaker than English "shall" (it is closer to "should"). A naive English-to-German mapping (shall to soll) changes legal meaning. requimd should treat "muss" as binding, "soll" as recommended and "wird" as intention, and cross-check with `prio`. "kann" or "darf" in a requirement is usually an error or a permission statement.
- **Verb-final order and separable verbs:** "Das System muss die Daten jede Nacht sichern" puts the process verb last. With "anzeigen" the particle may split in main clauses ("zeigt ... an"), but within the modal construction the infinitive stays whole. A "required verb" check in German should look for modal plus clause-final infinitive.
- **Passive:** "werden" plus Partizip II ("Die Daten werden gespeichert") hides the actor (SOPHIST deletion). "wird" is ambiguous: it is the future-tense priority marker in Rupp and also the passive auxiliary. Disambiguate by looking for a participle (ge-...-t/-en) at the end.
- **Nominalisations:** nouns ending in -ung, -heit, -keit or -ion used as process ("Die Durchführung der Prüfung erfolgt ...") are a classic SOPHIST deletion. Flag "erfolgt", "findet statt" and "durchgeführt werden" combined with a nominalisation.
- **Weak words (German list):** möglichst, ggf./gegebenenfalls, i.d.R./in der Regel, etc./usw., u.a., bzw., und/oder, schnell, benutzerfreundlich, einfach, intuitiv, angemessen, ausreichend, zeitnah, regelmäßig, optimal, flexibel, mindestens/höchstens without a number, "sollte" (subjunctive), "normalerweise", "evtl.", "sofern möglich", "unter Umständen".
- **Glossary matching:** German inflection (Kunde, Kunden, Kundin) and compounds (Kundenkonto contains Kunde) require lemma or prefix matching plus a user-maintained list of forms in the glossary entry. Gender-inclusive forms (Kund*innen, Kund:innen) must be handled.
- **EARS in German:** there is no fixed official German EARS. A practical mapping is Solange (While), Wenn (When), Falls ... dann (If-then; this matches Rupp's "falls" for logical conditions), Sofern/Wo (Where). Clause order works, but the German verb-second rule moves the subject after a leading clause ("Wenn X, muss das System Y"), so the parser must accept "muss das System" after a condition.
- **Gherkin German keywords** exist (Angenommen, Wenn, Dann, Und, Aber) **[background]**, so acceptance-criteria checks can be language-aware.
- **Document vocabulary:** Lastenheft (customer requirements, roughly StRS) and Pflichtenheft (supplier solution description, roughly SyRS/SRS) are common in German industry and contracts **[background; DIN 69901-5 / VDI 2519 definitions not verified]**. A requimd profile could map item types to these.
- **Decimal comma and units:** "1,5 s" vs "1.5 s" (GtWR R40) is locale-dependent, and the check should follow the file language.

### Gaps
- The current official SOPHIST MASTeR template set (FunktionsMASTeR etc.) and its exact slot definitions were not found in open sources; the SOPHIST site has only book and training material.
- No public German weak-word list from IREB or SOPHIST was found; the list above is compiled from practice and needs validation by the owner.
